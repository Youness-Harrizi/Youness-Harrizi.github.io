**🚀 Executive Summary**
Active Directory (AD) is the nervous system of 90% of enterprise networks. For a pentester, understanding AD's schema, authentication protocols (Kerberos vs NTLM), and object hierarchy is not optional—it is the prerequisite for moving laterally and escalating privileges.

## 1. What is Active Directory?

At its core, Active Directory is a **hierarchical directory service**. It acts as the central authority that stores network object data and manages authentication/authorization.

<div class="grid-container" markdown="1">
<div class="card" markdown="1">
**📂 Directory Structure** Hierarchical organization of all network resources (Forests, Trees, Domains, OUs).
</div>

<div class="card" markdown="1">
**🔐 Authentication** The central referee for identity. It decides who is who (Kerberos/NTLM).
</div>

<div class="card" markdown="1">
**👥 Account Mgmt** Centralized control of Users, Computers, and Service Accounts.
</div>

<div class="card" markdown="1">
**🛡️ Policy (GPO)** Configuration enforcement and security baselines pushed to endpoints.
</div>

## 2. Core Terminology (The Target Language)

To enumerate a domain effectively, you must understand the addressing system.

### Naming Conventions

| Type | Format | Context |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **FQDN** | `W10-X.CORP.LOCAL` | Complete DNS name; follows forest structure. |
| **DN** | `CN=User,OU=Dev,DC=corp...` | The unique LDAP path to an object. |
| **SAM** | `DOMAIN\User` | Legacy "Pre-Windows 2000" login format. |
| **UPN** | `user@corp.local` | Modern, email-style login format. |
